# Discussion: What the Corpus Can and Cannot Claim {#sec:discussion}

The shared-core/application-policy boundary makes reuse reviewable. Different
applications can consume the same pinned source evidence while declaring different
query vocabularies, retrieval profiles and review policies. Agreement in byte
custody does not imply agreement in interpretation. Likewise, a reusable hierarchy
layout is a mechanism for displaying declared relationships; the application must
justify those relationships independently. The profile diagnostic therefore
documents computational choices rather than presenting shared software as a
validation of any literary or disciplinary conclusion.

The main result is a connected account of corpus construction, local custody, and bounded analysis. The {{WORKS_PRESENT}} source/copy records map to {{TARGETS_REPRESENTED}} represented ledger targets; {{TARGETS_PRESENT}} meet the full declared evidence profile. Those are different quantities. Copy repetition, source authority, authorship, missing modalities, and rights decisions remain visible instead of being collapsed into a download count [@atkins1992corpus; @biber1993representativeness; @mceneryhardie2012corpus; @price2009edition; @sahle2016sde].

The exploratory language results describe {{WORDS_TOTAL}} tokenizer-defined words across {{WORKS_WITH_TEXT}} text-bearing records. They include transcriptions with unverified authorship and must not be interpreted as Blake-wide vocabulary, sentiment, or entity estimates. A separate primary-scope search requires verified reading-text roles. This separation lets uncertain material remain recoverable and inspectable without silently promoting it into primary evidence [@pechenick2015googlebooks; @bode2018world; @piper2018enumerations; @underwood2019distant].

Preservation adds a complementary denominator. A source inventory can be fully recoverable relative to its declared captured files while image acquisition remains incomplete and whole-oeuvre coverage remains unknown. The backup checksum and the failed-before-repaired corruption control test byte custody. They do not test scholarly completeness or transcription accuracy. Reusing original images while analyzing an independent metadata store preserves that distinction across analysis epochs [@owens2018digitalpreservation; @kirschenbaum2008mechanisms; @w3cprov2013overview].

The computational figures are instruments for review and close reading. Vocabulary projections expose transcription and selection effects; theme links and entity strings suggest places to inspect. They do not establish literary periodization, iconographic truth, or a settled semantic graph. In particular, spaCy performs pretrained statistical named-entity recognition. Its outputs are not model-free, and this project supplies no Blake-specific accuracy validation. Future multimodal, optical-character-recognition, and knowledge-graph experiments require their own evidence and evaluation [@ramsay2011readingmachines; @rockwellsinclair2016hermeneutica; @drucker2011graphical; @drucker2020visualization; @greif2025multimodal; @rei2024multimodal; @li2025musekg; @zhang2026multimodal].

Publication custody is part of the method. The generated cover is a labeled design asset; aggregate figures are project-authored analytical displays. Provider-image mosaics remain private. The public release distributes software, manuscript sources, aggregate evidence, and checksums, while preserving the distinction between the project license and source-provider rights [@candela_collections_data; @padilla2019santabarbara]. Publication identifies a reproducible artifact set; it is not peer review or a declaration of completed Blake scholarship.

The remaining work follows the evidence ledger: resolve partial and unverified targets, improve source/copy classification and dating, review reading-text authorship, investigate unresolved image references, and evaluate model outputs against editorial judgments. New acquisition must record current source identity and availability rather than inherit the endpoint-health claims of an older epoch [@reed2014managing; @klein2014reference; @vandesompel2013memento].
